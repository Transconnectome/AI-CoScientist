## 1. SUMMARY ASSESSMENT

**Scientific Merit Score:** 6.5/10

This study addresses a clinically important question using rigorous methodology (drug-naïve sample, standardized preprocessing, appropriate statistical corrections). The focus on sensorimotor circuit connectivity as a predictor of SSRI response in OCD is mechanistically plausible and represents an incremental advance. However, the work is fundamentally limited by: (1) absence of external validation despite very high prediction metrics (AUC 0.82-0.86, R²=0.48) that raise overfitting concerns; (2) sample size (N=54) below contemporary expectations for validation studies as discussed by Marek & Laumann (2025, NPP 50:52-57); (3) cross-sectional design preventing mechanistic inference; and (4) lack of comparison to clinical predictors, undermining biomarker utility claims.

**Fit for NPP Score:** 6/10

The manuscript aligns with NPP's emphasis on translational neuroimaging but falls short of contemporary editorial priorities emphasizing external validation, larger samples, and demonstrated clinical utility beyond existing predictors (see Barch & Liston editorials on reproducibility in neuroimaging).

**Overall Recommendation:** **Major Revisions**

**Rationale:** The study meets fundamental scientific standards and addresses an important clinical need, but requires substantial reframing as exploratory work, addition of internal cross-validation analyses, comparison to clinical predictors, and explicit acknowledgment of generalizability limitations before acceptance.

## 2. MAJOR STRENGTHS

### Strength 1: Exemplary Sample Characteristics and Study Design
**Evidence:** 100% drug-naïve OCD patients (Table 1), eliminating medication confounds that plague most neuroimaging studies in this population.

**Importance:** This represents an **exceptional design feature** that substantially strengthens causal inference. The ENIGMA consortium has identified medication status as a critical confound in OCD neuroimaging meta-analyses. By studying treatment-naïve patients, the authors avoid the circular reasoning inherent in studies where baseline connectivity may already reflect prior medication exposure.

**Contemporary Context:** Drug-naïve samples are increasingly rare and highly valued in neuropsychopharmacology research. This design feature partially compensates for the single-site limitation and positions the work as a clean test of the predictive hypothesis.

### Strength 2: Rigorous Preprocessing and Quality Control
**Evidence:** CONN Toolbox 22.v with SPM12, ART-based scrubbing (FD >0.9mm threshold), CompCor nuisance regression, appropriate bandpass filtering (0.017-0.1 Hz), explicit exclusion criteria for motion artifacts (5 patients, 7 controls excluded).

**Importance:** The preprocessing pipeline meets contemporary best practices and exceeds many published studies. Motion artifact control is particularly critical given that head motion correlates with clinical severity in psychiatric populations and can generate spurious connectivity findings.

**Specific Excellence:** The authors report both voxel-level (p<0.001) and cluster-level FWE correction (p<0.05), addressing multiple comparisons more conservatively than many contemporary studies that rely solely on FDR correction.

### Strength 3: Mechanistically Grounded ROI Selection
**Evidence:** The sensorimotor circuit was selected a priori based on: (1) ENIGMA mega-analysis showing widespread hypoconnectivity in OCD; (2) dense serotonergic innervation from raphe nuclei; (3) association with core OCD features (habitual behaviors, sensory phenomena, response inhibition deficits).

**Importance:** This represents a **theory-driven approach** that contrasts favorably with purely data-driven whole-brain searches. The convergence of pathophysiological relevance (OCD-implicated circuit) and pharmacological plausibility (serotonergic modulation target) provides a coherent scientific rationale.

**Contemporary Alignment:** Recent NPP publications increasingly prioritize mechanistic insights alongside clinical utility. The sensorimotor circuit hypothesis bridges neurobiology and treatment prediction more effectively than atheoretical biomarker discovery approaches.

### Strength 4: Convergent Evidence from Independent Research
**Evidence:** The authors appropriately contextualize their findings relative to Wang et al. (2025, *J Affect Disord*), which used a different method (zfALFF vs. FC) but reached similar conclusions about sensorimotor abnormalities predicting SSRI response.

**Importance:** Cross-method replication substantially mitigates concerns about method-specific artifacts and strengthens the sensorimotor hypothesis. This convergence partially addresses the absence of external validation within the current manuscript.

**Impact:** The consistency across independent samples and analytical approaches suggests the finding may be robust, though formal meta-analysis would be needed to quantify effect size stability.

### Strength 5: Comprehensive Reporting and Transparency
**Evidence:** Detailed methods enabling replication, complete demographic/clinical tables, supplementary materials with full statistical results, explicit statement of limitations, data availability upon reasonable request.

**Importance:** Meets fundamental requirements for transparent methodology. The level of detail provided (e.g., exact scanner parameters, preprocessing steps, statistical thresholds) allows independent researchers to attempt replication—a critical feature given the replication crisis in neuroimaging.

**Reproducibility:** The use of widely available tools (CONN, SPM12) and standard atlases (Kong 2022, Harvard-Oxford) facilitates reproducibility across sites.

## 3. MAJOR CONCERNS

### Concern 1: Absence of External Validation with Implausibly High Prediction Metrics
**Issue:** The reported AUC values (0.82-0.86) and R² values (up to 0.479) are exceptionally high for neuroimaging prediction, particularly given the small sample (N=54). The extreme odds ratios (OR: 0.001 to 70,000) with very wide confidence intervals suggest model instability.

**Impact on Validity:** **CRITICAL**. Meta-analyses of brain-behavior associations show typical effect sizes of r=0.1-0.3 (R²=0.01-0.09) in adequately powered samples. The current findings exceed this by an order of magnitude, raising substantial overfitting concerns. Without cross-validation, these metrics likely represent optimistic upper bounds rather than generalizable performance.

**Evidence Base:** Marek & Laumann (2025, NPP 50:52-57) demonstrate that small samples (N<100) show high false positive rates, with effect sizes inflating dramatically. Their analysis of brain-wide association studies reveals that samples below N=100 typically yield effect sizes 2-3 times larger than those observed in adequately powered replication samples.

**Specific Recommendation:** 
- **ESSENTIAL:** Conduct leave-one-out cross-validation (LOOCV) or 10-fold cross-validation to obtain unbiased estimates of prediction accuracy
- **ESSENTIAL:** Report cross-validated AUC, sensitivity, specificity, positive/negative predictive values
- **ESSENTIAL:** Add permutation testing (1000+ iterations) to establish null distribution for prediction accuracy
- **ESSENTIAL:** Reframe all claims as "exploratory candidate biomarker requiring external validation" rather than implying clinical readiness

### Concern 2: No Comparison to Clinical Predictors
**Issue:** The manuscript claims sensorimotor FC predicts treatment response but provides no comparison to clinical variables alone (baseline Y-BOCS, illness duration, age of onset, symptom dimensions) or combined models (imaging + clinical).

**Impact on Validity:** **CRITICAL**. Without demonstrating incremental predictive value beyond readily available clinical information, the clinical utility of an 8-minute MRI scan remains unestablished. This represents a fundamental gap in biomarker validation studies.

**Clinical Translation Gap:** The authors state "no clinical variables showed significant predictive value" (Results, Predicting treatment response section), but this finding is presented without adequate analysis. Simple univariate tests may miss multivariate clinical signatures. More importantly, even if clinical variables show lower individual AUC values, a combined model might outperform imaging alone.

**Specific Recommendation:**
- **ESSENTIAL:** Build logistic regression models with clinical predictors (baseline Y-BOCS, illness duration, age, sex, education)
- **ESSENTIAL:** Compare AUC values: clinical-only vs. imaging-only vs. combined model
- **ESSENTIAL:** Report net reclassification improvement (NRI) and integrated discrimination improvement (IDI) statistics
- **STRONGLY RECOMMENDED:** Conduct decision curve analysis to quantify net benefit at different decision thresholds
- **STRONGLY RECOMMENDED:** Discuss cost-effectiveness: Does the added predictive value justify MRI acquisition costs?

### Concern 3: Sample Size Below Contemporary Expectations for Validation Studies
**Issue:** N=54 patients (33 responders, 21 non-responders) falls below sample sizes typically seen in recent neuroimaging validation studies. Marek & Laumann (2025) recommend N≥100 as a minimum for establishing reliable brain-behavior associations, while acknowledging that even larger samples (N>1000) may be needed for small effects.

**Impact on Validity:** **MODERATE to CRITICAL**. The unbalanced group sizes (33 vs. 21) further reduce statistical power for the smaller non-responder group. Confidence intervals for prediction metrics will be wide, limiting precision of effect size estimates.

**Context:** Recent OCD treatment prediction studies have achieved larger samples (e.g., N=177 multi-site study cited in calibration document), demonstrating feasibility. The single-site design, while offering scanner consistency, sacrifices generalizability.

**Specific Recommendation:**
- **ESSENTIAL:** Explicitly acknowledge sample size limitation in Abstract, Results, and Discussion
- **ESSENTIAL:** Reframe study as "exploratory discovery" rather than "validation"
- **STRONGLY RECOMMENDED:** Conduct sensitivity analyses with different response thresholds (25%, 50% Y-BOCS reduction) to assess robustness
- **STRONGLY RECOMMENDED:** Provide power calculations for future validation studies based on observed effect sizes
- **OPTIONAL:** Pursue multi-site collaboration for external validation (acknowledge in Discussion as next step)

### Concern 4: Cross-Sectional Design Limits Mechanistic Claims
**Issue:** The manuscript makes mechanistic claims about SSRI modulation of sensorimotor circuits (e.g., "SSRIs may exert their therapeutic effects... through gradual recalibration of sensory-motor coordination") without post-treatment imaging data.

**Impact on Validity:** **MODERATE**. Cross-sectional baseline differences between responders and non-responders cannot establish whether: (1) FC changes with treatment, (2) baseline FC causally influences response, or (3) both reflect a third variable (e.g., illness severity, neural reserve).

**Conflation of Prediction and Mechanism:** The Discussion conflates predictive utility (baseline FC discriminates groups) with mechanistic understanding (FC mediates treatment effects). These are distinct scientific questions requiring different designs.

**Specific Recommendation:**
- **ESSENTIAL:** Remove or substantially hedge mechanistic claims throughout (Abstract: "may contribute to"; Discussion: "we hypothesize" rather than "our findings suggest")
- **ESSENTIAL:** Add explicit statement: "The cross-sectional design precludes causal inference about whether baseline FC differences reflect preserved neural capacity, compensatory mechanisms, or distinct illness subtypes"
- **STRONGLY RECOMMENDED:** Acknowledge in Limitations: "Longitudinal imaging with pre- and post-treatment scans is needed to test whether sensorimotor FC changes mediate treatment response"
- **OPTIONAL:** Reframe as "prognostic biomarker" (predicts outcome) rather than "mechanistic biomarker" (explains mechanism)

### Concern 5: Generalizability Limitations Inadequately Addressed
**Issue:** Single-site (Shanghai), single-scanner (3T Siemens), single-ethnicity (Chinese), single-medication (sertraline), single-dosing schedule design severely limits generalizability, yet these constraints receive minimal discussion.

**Impact on Validity:** **MODERATE**. Scanner-specific artifacts, population-specific brain anatomy, and medication-specific effects could all limit applicability to other settings. The title claims "SSRI responsiveness" but only sertraline was tested.

**Contemporary Emphasis:** Recent NPP editorials increasingly emphasize multi-site collaborations and reproducibility as priorities for the field. Single-site studies remain acceptable if appropriately framed but require explicit acknowledgment of boundaries.

**Specific Recommendation:**
- **ESSENTIAL:** Revise title to specify "sertraline" rather than "SSRIs" (or add caveat "selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors" → "sertraline, a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor")
- **ESSENTIAL:** Expand Limitations section with dedicated paragraph on generalizability constraints
- **STRONGLY RECOMMENDED:** Add to Discussion: "Whether these findings extend to other SSRIs (fluoxetine, fluvoxamine, paroxetine), other populations (Western, African, etc.), or other scanners requires empirical validation"
- **STRONGLY RECOMMENDED:** Discuss potential moderators: "Factors such as symptom dimensions (contamination vs. symmetry), comorbidity profiles, and illness chronicity may influence the predictive utility of sensorimotor FC"

## 4. METHODOLOGICAL EVALUATION

### Preprocessing: **Excellent (9/10)**
The CONN Toolbox pipeline with ART-based scrubbing, CompCor nuisance regression, and appropriate filtering represents state-of-the-art preprocessing. The explicit motion exclusion criteria (FD >0.9mm) and reporting of excluded subjects (5 patients, 7 controls) demonstrate rigor. 

**Minor suggestion:** Report mean FD values for each group to rule out residual group differences in motion.

### Statistical Approach: **Adequate but Limited (6/10)**
The seed-based FC analysis with FWE cluster-level correction is appropriate and conservative. However, the univariate logistic regression approach is simpler than the multivariate machine learning methods increasingly common in recent neuroimaging publications.

**Justification for univariate approach:** Given N=54, complex machine learning models would be prone to severe overfitting. The authors' choice is defensible but should be explicitly justified.

**Critical gap:** No cross-validation of any kind. Even simple LOOCV would substantially strengthen credibility.

**Recommendation:** Add internal cross-validation and justify univariate approach: "Given the sample size (N=54), we employed univariate logistic regression rather than multivariate machine learning to minimize overfitting risk. However, external validation in larger samples will be essential to confirm these findings."

### Sample Characteristics: **Excellent (9/10)**
Drug-naïve status, matched controls, standardized diagnostic procedures (DSM-5, Y-BOCS ≥20), and clear response criteria (≥35% reduction) represent exemplary design. The 12-week treatment duration aligns with clinical guidelines.

**Minor concern:** No reporting of comorbidity rates (anxiety, depression) beyond exclusion of severe depression (BDI ≥29). Comorbidity could moderate prediction accuracy.

### Validation Status: **Poor (2/10)**
No external validation, no internal cross-validation, no replication sample. This is the manuscript's most critical weakness relative to contemporary expectations for predictive biomarker studies.

### Reporting Completeness: **Good (8/10)**
Sufficient detail for replication, though some elements could be enhanced:
- **Missing:** Mean FD values per group
- **Missing:** Comorbidity rates
- **Missing:** Medication adherence rates/final doses achieved
- **Missing:** Attrition analysis (were excluded subjects different from included?)
- **Present:** Scanner parameters, preprocessing steps, statistical thresholds, atlas details

## 5. SCIENTIFIC CONTRIBUTION ASSESSMENT

### Novelty: **Moderate (6/10)**
The sensorimotor circuit focus is relatively novel compared to prior OCD treatment prediction studies emphasizing raphe nuclei or cerebellum. However, the seed-based FC approach is standard, and the finding of baseline connectivity differences between responders/non-responders has been reported in other circuits (DAN-FPN, CSTC).

**Incremental advance:** The work extends existing knowledge by identifying a specific circuit with mechanistic plausibility but does not represent a paradigm shift.

### Significance: **Moderate to High (7/10)**
**Clinical importance:** Addressing 50% SSRI non-response rate is highly significant. If validated, a baseline biomarker could reduce disability burden and healthcare costs.

**Scientific impact:** The convergence with Wang et al. (2025) using different methods strengthens the sensorimotor hypothesis and may redirect research attention from traditional CSTC circuits.

**Limitation:** Without external validation or clinical utility demonstration, the immediate impact is limited to hypothesis generation.

### Rigor: **Moderate (6/10)**
**Strengths:** Excellent preprocessing, appropriate corrections, drug-naïve sample, transparent reporting.

**Weaknesses:** No validation, no comparison to clinical predictors, overfitting concerns, cross-sectional design limiting mechanistic inference.

**Overall assessment:** Rigorous execution of an exploratory study, but insufficient rigor for definitive biomarker claims.

### Impact Potential: **Moderate (6/10)**
**Positive indicators:**
- Addresses important clinical problem
- Feasible implementation (8-min rs-fMRI widely available)
- Mechanistically interpretable
- Convergent evidence from independent work

**Limiting factors:**
- Requires validation before clinical translation
- Unclear incremental value over clinical predictors
- Single-site, single-medication, single-population limits generalizability
- No clear pathway to implementation (cost-effectiveness unknown)

**Likely trajectory:** Will stimulate replication attempts and may contribute to multi-site meta-analyses, but unlikely to immediately change clinical practice.

## 7. SPECIFIC RECOMMENDATIONS

### ESSENTIAL (must address for acceptance):

1. **Add Internal Cross-Validation Analysis**
   - Conduct leave-one-out cross-validation (LOOCV) for all logistic regression models
   - Report cross-validated AUC, sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV with 95% CI
   - Add permutation testing (≥1000 iterations) to establish null distribution
   - **Rationale:** Without validation, the reported AUC values (0.82-0.86) are likely inflated and not generalizable (Marek & Laumann 2025)

2. **Compare to Clinical Predictors**
   - Build three models: (A) clinical variables only, (B) FC only, (C) combined
   - Clinical variables: baseline Y-BOCS, illness duration, age, sex, education, BDI score
   - Report comparative AUC values with DeLong test for statistical comparison
   - Calculate net reclassification improvement (NRI) and integrated discrimination improvement (IDI)
   - **Rationale:** Biomarker utility requires demonstrating added value beyond existing clinical information

3. **Reframe Claims as Exploratory**
   - Abstract: Change "biomarker" → "candidate biomarker requiring validation"
   - Abstract: Add "in an exploratory single-site study"
   - Discussion: Add paragraph explicitly stating exploratory nature and validation requirements
   - Remove or substantially hedge mechanistic claims (see Concern 4)
   - **Rationale:** N=54 without external validation is insufficient for definitive biomarker claims

4. **Expand Limitations Section**
   - Add dedicated paragraph on generalizability constraints (single-site, single-scanner, single-ethnicity, single-medication)
   - Acknowledge sample size limitation relative to contemporary recommendations (Marek & Laumann 2025)
   - Explicitly state that cross-sectional design precludes causal/mechanistic inference
   - Discuss potential for overfitting given high AUC values on small sample
   - **Rationale:** Current limitations section is insufficient given the scope of constraints

5. **Revise Title for Accuracy**
   - Current: "...responsiveness to selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors..."
   - Proposed: "...responsiveness to sertraline in obsessive-compulsive disorder: an exploratory study"
   - **Rationale:** Only sertraline was tested; title should not overgeneralize to all SSRIs

6. **Report Residual Motion Parameters**
   - Add table with mean FD, mean DVARS, percentage of scrubbed volumes for each group
   - Conduct ANOVA to verify no group differences in residual motion
   - **Rationale:** Motion artifacts can confound group differences; readers need assurance of adequate control

### STRONGLY RECOMMENDED (should address for strong paper):

7. **Conduct Sensitivity Analyses**
   - Test alternative response thresholds: 25%, 50% Y-BOCS reduction
   - Examine whether predictive patterns hold across different cutoffs
   - Report how many patients fall into each category at each threshold
   - **Rationale:** The 35% threshold is conventional but somewhat arbitrary; robustness across thresholds strengthens findings

8. **Add Decision Curve Analysis**
   - Calculate net benefit at different decision thresholds
   - Compare to "treat all" and "treat none" strategies
   - Visualize with decision curve plot
   - **Rationale:** Demonstrates clinical utility by quantifying benefit at realistic decision points

9. **Provide Power Calculations for Future Studies**
   - Based on observed effect sizes, calculate required N for adequately powered validation
   - Specify target power (0.80 or 0.90), alpha (0.05), and expected effect size
   - **Rationale:** Guides future research and demonstrates awareness of current study's limitations

10. **Report Medication Adherence and Dosing**
    - Provide distribution of final sertraline doses achieved
    - Report adherence rates (e.g., percentage of prescribed doses taken)
    - Analyze whether dose or adherence moderates prediction accuracy
    - **Rationale:** Treatment response depends on adequate dosing and adherence; these are potential confounds

11. **Analyze Comorbidity as Moderator**
    - Report rates of comorbid anxiety, depression (below exclusion threshold)
    - Test whether comorbidity moderates the FC-response relationship
    - **Rationale:** Comorbidity is common in OCD and may influence treatment response and neural signatures

12. **Justify Univariate Statistical Approach**
    - Add paragraph explaining why univariate logistic regression was chosen over machine learning
    - Cite literature on overfitting risk in small samples with high-dimensional data
    - **Rationale:** Readers may question why contemporary ML methods were not used; proactive justification strengthens manuscript

### OPTIONAL ENHANCEMENTS (would strengthen paper):

13. **Provide Code and Processed Data**
    - Share preprocessing scripts, analysis code (CONN batch scripts, SPSS syntax)
    - Deposit processed FC matrices (anonymized) in public repository
    - **Rationale:** Enhances reproducibility and allows independent verification; aligns with open science principles

14. **Conduct Symptom Dimension Analysis**
    - Use Y-BOCS symptom checklist to categorize patients by dominant symptom dimension
    - Test whether predictive patterns differ across dimensions (contamination, symmetry, hoarding, etc.)
    - **Rationale:** OCD is heterogeneous; dimension-specific biomarkers may have greater clinical utility

15. **Add Cerebellar Parcellation Analysis**
    - Given unexpected cerebellar findings, conduct more detailed parcellation
    - Distinguish sensorimotor (lobules I-VI) from cognitive (Crus I/II, VIIb) cerebellar regions
    - Test whether sensorimotor vs. cognitive cerebellar connectivity shows differential prediction
    - **Rationale:** Strengthens cerebellar findings and provides more specific mechanistic hypotheses

16. **Compare to Published Prediction Models**
    - Systematically compare current findings to prior OCD treatment prediction studies
    - Create table summarizing: study, N, method, predictors, AUC/accuracy
    - Position current work within this landscape
    - **Rationale:** Contextualizes contribution and helps readers assess relative performance

17. **Propose Multi-Site Validation Protocol**
    - In Discussion, outline specific plan for external validation
    - Specify target sample size, sites, inclusion criteria, analysis plan
    - Consider pre-registration
    - **Rationale:** Demonstrates commitment to validation and provides roadmap for future work

## 8. DECISION RATIONALE

### Evidence-Based Justification for Major Revisions

**Fundamental Compliance:** The manuscript meets all 5 fundamental criteria, establishing scientific validity and ethical conduct.

**Contemporary Expectations:** The manuscript meets exactly 50% (4/8) of contemporary expectations for validation studies, placing it at the decision boundary. Critical gaps include:
- No external validation (most serious deficiency)
- No comparison to clinical predictors (undermines biomarker utility claims)
- Sample size below recommendations for establishing reliable associations (N=54 vs. N≥100 recommended by Marek & Laumann 2025)
- Cross-sectional design limiting mechanistic claims

**Excellence Indicators:** Only 2/7 excellence markers present (drug-naïve sample, convergent evidence), indicating the work does not reach the highest tier of contemporary publications.

**Balancing Factors:**

*Strengths favoring acceptance:*
1. Addresses clinically important problem (50% SSRI non-response)
2. Exemplary methodological rigor (preprocessing, motion control, statistical corrections)
3. Drug-naïve sample eliminates major confound
4. Mechanistically plausible hypothesis (sensorimotor circuit)
5. Convergent evidence from independent study (Wang et al. 2025)
6. Feasible implementation pathway (8-min rs-fMRI)

*Weaknesses favoring rejection:*
1. No external validation with implausibly high prediction metrics (AUC 0.82-0.86)
2. No internal cross-validation (even LOOCV would help)
3. No comparison to clinical predictors (fundamental gap for biomarker claims)
4. Sample size below contemporary recommendations for validation studies
5. Generalizability severely limited (single-site, single-medication, single-population)

### Path Forward for Authors

**Major Revisions are recommended because:**

1. **Core scientific contribution is sound:** The sensorimotor circuit hypothesis is mechanistically grounded, the execution is rigorous, and the findings converge with independent evidence.

2. **Critical gaps are addressable without new data collection:** Internal cross-validation, comparison to clinical predictors, sensitivity analyses, and reframing of claims can all be accomplished with existing data.

3. **Clinical importance justifies publication:** Even as exploratory work, these findings will stimulate validation efforts and contribute to the growing literature on OCD treatment prediction.

4. **Resubmission potential is high:** If authors address the essential recommendations, the manuscript would meet contemporary standards for exploratory biomarker discovery studies.

**What authors must do for acceptance:**

**Minimum requirements (all essential recommendations):**
- Add internal cross-validation (LOOCV or k-fold)
- Compare FC predictors to clinical predictors and combined models
- Reframe all claims as exploratory/candidate biomarker requiring validation
- Expand limitations section to adequately address generalizability constraints
- Revise title to specify sertraline (not all SSRIs)
- Report residual motion parameters

**Strongly encouraged (would substantially strengthen manuscript):**
- Conduct sensitivity analyses with alternative response thresholds
- Add decision curve analysis
- Provide power calculations for future validation studies
- Report medication adherence and dosing details
- Analyze comorbidity as potential moderator
- Justify univariate statistical approach

### Contemporary Standards Alignment

**Where the paper meets expectations:**
- Preprocessing methodology (exceeds typical standards)
- Statistical corrections (meets expectations)
- Sample size for exploratory work (meets minimum N≥50)
- Clinical relevance (clearly demonstrated)
- Transparent reporting (adequate for replication)

**Where the paper falls short:**
- External validation (increasingly expected in recent publications, absent here)
- Sample size for establishing reliable associations (N=54 vs. N≥100 recommended by Marek & Laumann 2025)
- Clinical utility demonstration (no comparison to existing predictors)
- Mechanistic inference (cross-sectional design insufficient)
- Generalizability (single-site, single-medication, single-population)

### Alternative Scenarios

**If authors add external validation dataset:** Would elevate recommendation to **Accept with Minor Revisions**, as this would address the most critical deficiency and substantially increase confidence in the findings.

**If authors cannot address essential recommendations:** Would change recommendation to **Reject with Encouragement to Resubmit** after obtaining validation data or conducting multi-site collaboration.

**If authors adequately address all essential recommendations:** Would support **Acceptance** as an important exploratory study that advances the field incrementally and sets the stage for definitive validation work.
