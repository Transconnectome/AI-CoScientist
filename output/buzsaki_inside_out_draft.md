## 2. **Draft Insertions for the Perspective**

### **Introduction**
Traditional neuroscience has often privileged perception as the entry point for understanding cognition, casting the brain as a passive recipient of sensory information. However, our inside-out framework contends that volitional behaviour precedes and sculpts perception. The brain does not wait for the world to write on its “blank slate”; rather, it actively generates predictions through self-initiated actions, using the resulting sensory consequences as feedback to refine its internal models ([InsideOut Ch.1], see ch1_The_Problem.pdf#73). This inversion is not merely philosophical—it is grounded in the dynamics of cell assemblies that encode the temporally-structured sequences of action, which are later internalized and repurposed for abstract cognition ([InsideOut Ch.7]). Thus, behaviour is not the endpoint of neural computation but its primordial learning signal, launching a cascade of prediction, comparison, and adaptation that underpins all higher cognitive functions.

### **Action as Delayed Thought**
We have argued that action is not simply a motor output but a form of delayed, embodied thinking. Each volitional movement—whether a saccade, a reach, or exploratory play—launches predictions about the world, which are then compared against sensory feedback to update neural assemblies ([InsideOut Ch.3], see ch3_Perception_from_Action.pdf#37). This mechanism is vividly illustrated by the mirror neuron system, where the same neural patterns are engaged during both the observation and execution of actions ([InsideOut Ch.5], see ch5_Internalization_of_ExperienceCognition_from_Action.pdf#64/75). Such internalization allows the brain to simulate and rehearse action sequences even in the absence of overt movement, effectively transforming behaviour into mental exploration. The reduction of sensory ambiguity through action—using the body to select, isolate, and test objects—further exemplifies how acting is thinking in motion ([InsideOut Ch.5], see ch5_Internalization_of_ExperienceCognition_from_Action.pdf#79).

### **Neonatal Neural Syntax and Meaning through Action**
From birth, the brain already resembles a dictionary whose pages are filled with internally generated “words” and syntactic rules, awaiting behavioural experiences to assign meaning ([InsideOut Ch.1], see ch1_The_Problem.pdf#73). Head-direction, border, and nascent grid cells provide a provisional spatial lexicon even before pups venture beyond the nest, while early spindle bursts map the body through stochastic self-movements ([InsideOut Ch.3], see ch3_Perception_from_Action.pdf#60). Exploratory action therefore does not create trajectories de novo; it aligns these preconfigured sequences with the statistics of the environment, turning nonsense syllables into grounded semantics. Hippocampal sharp-wave ripples subsequently replay these fledgling matches, cementing the dialogue between innate neural syntax and lived experience ([InsideOut Ch.8], see ch8_Internally_Organized_Activity_During_Offline_Brain_States.pdf#4). Behaviour is thus the bridge marrying an inherited neuronal lexicon to the semantics of the world.

### **Embodied Predictive Coding Across Scales**
The predictive hierarchies of the brain are seeded and shaped by action-generated sequences. At the level of cell assemblies, repeated behaviours are encoded as temporally-structured trajectories, which can be flexibly replayed and recombined to support increasingly abstract forms of inference ([InsideOut Ch.7], see ch7_Internally_Organized_Cell_Assembly_Trajectories.pdf#68). This principle extends from the hippocampal representation of spatial navigation to the parsing of birdsong and grooming routines, illustrating a scale-invariant logic of action-derived prediction. The philosopher Hegel’s notion of the transformation of quantity into quality finds a neural correlate here: as assemblies are mobilized and recombined, qualitative shifts in cognition emerge from the accumulation of action-encoded sequences ([InsideOut Ch.12], see ch12_Everything_Is_a_RelationshipThe_Nonegalitarian_LogScaled_Brain.pdf#73).

### **Rest & Replay**
Rest and sleep are not passive states but critical windows for the consolidation and abstraction of action-derived models. Hippocampal sharp-wave ripples (SWRs) replay sequences of cell assembly activity that were first generated during self-initiated behaviour ([InsideOut Ch.8], see ch8_Internally_Organized_Activity_During_Offline_Brain_States.pdf#4). This offline replay enables the transfer of action-encoded trajectories to neocortical circuits, supporting the formation of flexible, generalizable cognitive maps ([InsideOut Ch.11], see ch11_Gain_and_Abstraction.pdf#41). Our experiments have shown that such replay is essential for the internalization of experience, allowing the brain to “think ahead” and simulate novel routes or solutions based on past actions. In this light, replay is the neural substrate of imagination and planning, rooted in the primordial logic of embodied action.

### **Implications**
The educational and translational implications of the inside-out perspective are profound. To foster robust predictive hierarchies, learning environments must be designed to encourage self-generated exploration and manipulation, rather than passive observation ([InsideOut Ch.9], see ch9_Enhancing_Brain_Performance_by_Externalizing_Thought.pdf#47). This principle extends from early development—where play and movement are the engines of neural refinement—to the engineering of artificial systems that learn from interaction rather than instruction. By respecting the brain’s evolutionary logic, we can optimize both natural and artificial intelligence, ensuring that prediction, abstraction, and creativity remain grounded in the primordial signal of volitional action ([InsideOut Ch.13], see ch13_The_Brains_Best_Guess.pdf#40).
