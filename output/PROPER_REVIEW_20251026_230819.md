# Remarks to be Sent to the Author

## Manuscript MS 37480: Sensorimotor Circuit Connectivity as SSRI Response Predictor in OCD

This manuscript addresses an important clinical question using rigorous methodology. The drug-naïve sample is particularly valuable, and the preprocessing procedures are exemplary. However, the manuscript requires **Major Revisions** to align with contemporary expectations for predictive biomarker studies. The fundamental science is sound, but substantial revisions are needed before the work can be accepted for publication.

The required revisions are organized into three sections: essential revisions that must be addressed for acceptance, strongly recommended additions that would significantly strengthen the paper, and optional enhancements the authors might consider. All essential revisions can be accomplished using the existing data.

---

## PART A: ESSENTIAL REVISIONS (Must Be Addressed for Acceptance)

### A1. Internal Cross-Validation is Required

The manuscript reports exceptionally high prediction metrics—AUC values of 0.82-0.86 and R²=0.48—without any form of cross-validation. These values almost certainly represent optimistic upper bounds that will not generalize to new patients. Without validation, the reported performance metrics are not credible, particularly in a sample of this size.

The authors must conduct leave-one-out cross-validation (LOOCV) for all logistic regression models. The revised manuscript should report the full suite of cross-validated metrics: AUC with 95% confidence intervals, sensitivity, specificity, positive and negative predictive values, and balanced accuracy (given the somewhat unbalanced groups: 33 responders vs 21 non-responders). Permutation testing with a minimum of 1,000 iterations should establish the null distribution for prediction accuracy. Both in-sample (original) and out-of-sample (cross-validated) metrics must be reported to allow readers to assess the degree of overfitting.

These cross-validated values must replace the current metrics throughout the manuscript, particularly in the Abstract where readers encounter them first. A new Results subsection titled "Cross-Validation Analysis" should be added after the current prediction section. Figure 3 should be updated to display both original and cross-validated ROC curves side-by-side, allowing direct visual comparison. The Discussion must interpret the cross-validated performance honestly and acknowledge any drop in accuracy from the original values.

The necessity of this revision is underscored by Marek & Laumann (2025, *Neuropsychopharmacology* 50:52-57), who demonstrate that small samples without validation show inflated effect sizes—typically 2-3 times larger than true effects. Cross-validation provides an unbiased estimate of how well the model would actually perform on new patients, which is essential for any claim of predictive utility.

The authors' response letter should describe the cross-validation procedure implemented, report all cross-validated metrics with confidence intervals, and discuss the implications if cross-validated AUC drops substantially from the original values.

---

### A2. Comparison to Clinical Predictors is Essential

The manuscript claims that sensorimotor FC predicts treatment response but provides no evidence that neuroimaging adds value beyond readily available clinical information such as baseline severity, illness duration, or demographics. This comparison is essential for establishing clinical utility—biomarker validation requires demonstrating incremental predictive value beyond what clinicians already know from basic patient information.

The authors must build and compare three logistic regression models. Model A (clinical-only) should include baseline Y-BOCS total score, illness duration, age, sex, education level, and BDI score. Model B (FC-only) should include the sensorimotor connectivity values from the current analysis. Model C (combined) should include both clinical variables and FC values. For each model, the revised manuscript should report AUC with 95% confidence intervals. DeLong tests must compare AUC values between all pairs of models to establish whether differences are statistically significant. To quantify incremental value, the authors should calculate Net Reclassification Improvement (NRI) and Integrated Discrimination Improvement (IDI) comparing the combined model to the clinical-only baseline. Nagelkerke R² should be reported for all three models to indicate variance explained.

A comparison table showing all three models side-by-side must be added to the Results section, along with a figure displaying ROC curves for all three models on the same plot for direct visual comparison. A new Results subsection titled "Comparison to Clinical Predictors" should precede the Discussion. The new table should be titled "Comparative Performance of Clinical, Neuroimaging, and Combined Prediction Models."

The Discussion must interpret the incremental predictive value honestly. If FC adds significant value beyond clinical predictors, this strengthens the case for clinical translation. If it does not, the authors must discuss what this means for the clinical utility of the proposed biomarker. Without this comparison, the clinical utility of the biomarker remains completely unestablished.

The authors' response letter should report all comparative statistics (AUC differences, DeLong test results, NRI, IDI), discuss whether FC adds significant incremental value beyond clinical predictors, and if FC does not add value, address the implications for clinical translation.

### A3. Claims Must Be Reframed as Exploratory Discovery

The manuscript currently makes definitive biomarker claims that are not supported by the study design. With 54 participants from a single site and no external validation, the work represents exploratory discovery rather than biomarker validation. The framing throughout the manuscript must be revised to reflect this reality.

In the Abstract, line 15 should change "biomarker" to "candidate biomarker." Line 18 should add a qualifying phrase such as "in an exploratory single-site study." Line 22 should change "may serve as" to "represents a promising candidate for." The final sentence should add language such as "pending validation in independent samples."

In the Introduction, the final paragraph describing study purpose must be reframed as "exploratory discovery" rather than "validation." An explicit statement should be added indicating that this study aims to identify candidate neural predictors that require subsequent validation. In the Results section, the heading "Predicting treatment response" should be changed to "Exploratory prediction of treatment response," and mechanistic language throughout should be appropriately hedged.

The Discussion requires a new paragraph immediately after the opening paragraph that explicitly addresses the exploratory nature of the work:

> "The exploratory nature of this study must be emphasized. The sample size (N=54) falls below contemporary recommendations for establishing reliable brain-behavior associations (N≥100; Marek & Laumann, 2025), and external validation is lacking. These findings should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating and requiring replication rather than definitive evidence of a clinical biomarker. Independent validation in larger, multi-site samples is essential before any clinical implementation can be considered."

Throughout the Discussion, confident assertions must be replaced with appropriately hedged language: "demonstrates" should become "suggests"; "establishes" should become "provides preliminary evidence for"; "biomarker" should become "candidate biomarker"; "predicts" should become "shows potential to predict." The sample size, lack of external validation, and single-site design all necessitate framing this as exploratory work rather than definitive validation.

The authors' response letter should confirm that all claims have been appropriately hedged, describe the specific changes made to reframe the work as exploratory, and demonstrate understanding of the distinction between exploratory discovery and validation studies.

### A4. Mechanistic Claims Must Be Removed or Hedged

The cross-sectional design—baseline scans only, with no post-treatment imaging—cannot support mechanistic claims about how SSRIs work. The manuscript contains multiple mechanistic assertions that must be identified and revised.

In the Abstract, line 24 currently states "SSRIs may exert therapeutic effects through recalibration of sensory-motor coordination." This should be revised to something like: "Baseline sensorimotor connectivity differences may reflect preserved neural capacity that enables treatment response." In Discussion paragraph 3, statements such as "These findings suggest SSRIs modulate..." must be removed and replaced with language such as: "We hypothesize that baseline connectivity differences may reflect different illness subtypes or neural reserve capacity. Longitudinal imaging is needed to test whether FC changes mediate treatment response." Throughout the Discussion, all mechanistic statements must include appropriate qualifiers such as "We speculate that..." or "One possible interpretation is..." The manuscript must clearly distinguish between prediction (what can be concluded from the data) and mechanism (what requires further study).

A new paragraph must be added to the Limitations section:

> "The cross-sectional design precludes causal or mechanistic inference. Baseline FC differences between future responders and non-responders could reflect: (1) different OCD subtypes with inherently different treatment trajectories, (2) variations in neural reserve or compensatory capacity, (3) pre-existing differences in illness severity or chronicity not captured by Y-BOCS scores, or (4) other unmeasured confounds. Whether these FC differences causally influence treatment response or merely correlate with response cannot be determined from this design. Longitudinal imaging with pre- and post-treatment scans is required to test whether FC changes mediate therapeutic effects and to distinguish prognostic biomarkers (predicting outcome) from mechanistic biomarkers (explaining how treatment works)."

The critical distinction is that cross-sectional baseline differences cannot establish mechanism. This is a prognostic marker study, not a mechanistic study. The authors' response letter should list all mechanistic claims identified and describe how each was revised, and should confirm understanding of the distinction between prognostic and mechanistic biomarkers.

### A5. Limitations Section Must Be Substantially Expanded

The current limitations section does not adequately address the generalizability constraints and methodological limitations of this work. Four new paragraphs must be added.

**Paragraph 1 - Generalizability:** "This study was conducted at a single site (Shanghai Mental Health Center) using a single 3T Siemens scanner, which limits generalizability to other scanners and acquisition protocols. All participants were of Chinese ethnicity, and whether these findings extend to other populations (Caucasian, African, Hispanic, etc.) remains unknown. Cross-cultural differences in brain structure, connectivity patterns, and symptom presentation may moderate the predictive utility of sensorimotor FC. Multi-site replication across diverse populations is essential before these findings can be considered generalizable."

**Paragraph 2 - Single Medication:** "Only sertraline (50-200 mg/day) was tested, preventing generalization to other SSRIs (fluoxetine, fluvoxamine, paroxetine, escitalopram) or other pharmacological treatments (clomipramine, SNRIs, augmentation strategies). Different SSRIs have partially distinct pharmacological profiles beyond serotonin reuptake inhibition, and the current findings may not extend to all serotonergic agents. The title and claims have been revised to specify sertraline rather than SSRIs generally."

**Paragraph 3 - Sample Size:** "The sample size (N=54 total, 33 responders vs. 21 non-responders) falls below contemporary recommendations for establishing reliable brain-behavior associations. Marek and Laumann (2025) recommend N≥100 as a minimum for validation studies, noting that smaller samples show inflated effect sizes and higher false positive rates. The unbalanced group sizes further reduce statistical power and precision for the smaller non-responder group. These results should be interpreted as exploratory findings requiring replication in larger, adequately powered samples."

**Paragraph 4 - Overfitting Risk:** "The originally reported AUC values (0.82-0.86) are exceptionally high for neuroimaging prediction, and cross-validation revealed [INSERT CROSS-VALIDATED VALUES]. The difference between in-sample and out-of-sample performance indicates some degree of overfitting, as expected in small samples. Even with cross-validation, the single-site sample size limits confidence in effect size estimates. External validation in independent samples is the only definitive way to establish true prediction accuracy."

These four comprehensive paragraphs must replace the existing brief limitations discussion in the Discussion section, Limitations subsection. Readers require a complete understanding of the generalizability boundaries and methodological constraints of the work.

The authors' response letter should confirm that all four paragraphs have been added, fill in the cross-validated values in paragraph four, and describe any additional limitations identified beyond those specified here.

### A6. Title Revision and Motion Parameters Required

Two separate issues must be addressed. First, the title claims "SSRIs" but only sertraline was tested. The title must be revised from "Baseline Sensorimotor Circuit Connectivity Predicts Responsiveness to Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors in Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder" to "Baseline Sensorimotor Circuit Connectivity Predicts Responsiveness to Sertraline in Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder: An Exploratory Study." Accuracy requires specifying the specific SSRI tested, and adding "An Exploratory Study" appropriately frames the work.

Second, residual head motion can confound group differences and must be reported. The authors must calculate for each participant: mean framewise displacement (FD), mean DVARS, and percentage of volumes scrubbed. A new supplementary table titled "Supplementary Table X: Residual Motion Parameters by Group" must be created with columns for Group (Responders/Non-responders), Mean FD, SD FD, Mean DVARS, SD DVARS, and percentage of scrubbed volumes. ANOVA results testing group differences must be included. The Methods section should add text such as: "Mean framewise displacement did not differ between future responders (M=0.XX, SD=0.XX) and non-responders (M=0.XX, SD=0.XX), F(1,52)=X.XX, p=X.XX, ensuring that group differences in connectivity were not confounded by motion artifacts. Detailed motion parameters are provided in Supplementary Table X."

The authors' response letter should confirm the title has been revised, report the motion parameter statistics, and confirm whether there are group differences in residual motion.

## PART B: STRONGLY RECOMMENDED REVISIONS (Should Address for Strong Paper)

### B1. Sensitivity Analysis Across Response Thresholds

The 35% Y-BOCS reduction threshold, while conventional, is somewhat arbitrary. Testing robustness across different thresholds would substantially strengthen confidence in the findings.

The authors should test three response definitions: liberal (≥25% Y-BOCS reduction), conventional (≥35% reduction, current approach), and stringent (≥50% reduction). For each threshold, the revised manuscript should report the number of responders versus non-responders, the AUC for sensorimotor FC prediction, and the optimal FC threshold that maximizes sensitivity plus specificity. A comparison table across all three thresholds would help readers assess whether findings depend critically on the 35% cutoff.

A new Results subsection titled "Sensitivity to Response Threshold" should be added, along with a new supplementary table called "Prediction Performance Across Response Definitions." The Discussion should interpret whether findings are robust across thresholds or threshold-dependent. If findings hold across multiple definitions of response, confidence in the results increases substantially. If findings are highly threshold-dependent, clinical applicability may be narrower than currently claimed.

The authors should indicate in their response whether this analysis was conducted and summarize key findings.

---

### B2. Decision Curve Analysis Should Be Added

AUC and accuracy metrics do not directly answer the clinically relevant question: "At what decision thresholds does FC-guided treatment selection improve outcomes compared to treating everyone or treating no one?"

The authors should conduct decision curve analysis (DCA) for the Clinical-only model, FC-only model, and Combined model. Net benefit should be calculated at threshold probabilities from 0% to 100%. A decision curve plot should show net benefit curves for all three models, plus "Treat all" and "Treat none" reference lines. The analysis should identify threshold ranges where each model provides net benefit, with clinical interpretation of at what decision thresholds FC-guided selection would actually be beneficial.

A new Results subsection titled "Clinical Utility Analysis" should be added, along with a new figure called "Decision Curves for Clinical, Neuroimaging, and Combined Models." The Discussion should interpret at which thresholds neuroimaging adds clinical value. Decision curve analysis quantifies clinical utility by showing net benefit at realistic decision points, which directly informs whether FC assessment would actually improve clinical decision-making in practice.

The authors should indicate in their response whether DCA was conducted and describe key findings.

---

### B3. Power Calculations for Future Validation Studies

Readers and future researchers require guidance on what sample sizes would be adequate for validation studies.

Based on the observed effect sizes—specifically the cross-validated AUC values—the authors should calculate the required sample size for 80% power to detect the effect at α=0.05, and also for 90% power. Both conservative (10% smaller effect) and optimistic (observed effect) scenarios should be considered. The calculations should be reported in the Discussion with language such as: "Based on the cross-validated AUC of X.XX, a future validation study would require N=XXX participants (80% power, α=0.05) to replicate this effect. Given potential effect size inflation in this exploratory sample, a more conservative sample size of N=XXX (assuming 10% effect reduction) is recommended."

This paragraph should be added after the limitations section in the Discussion and should describe implications for multi-site validation efforts. This guidance would inform resource allocation for validation work and demonstrates awareness of the current study's power limitations.

The authors should provide power calculations in their response or explain if this is infeasible.

---

### B4. Medication Adherence and Dosing Details Should Be Reported

Treatment response depends on adequate medication exposure, and variability in adherence or final doses could confound prediction analyses.

The authors should report the distribution of final sertraline doses achieved—a histogram or frequency table showing dose ranges, mean, median, and range of final doses, plus the percentage of patients reaching each dose level (50mg, 100mg, 150mg, 200mg). If adherence data are available, the manuscript should report the method of adherence assessment (self-report, pill counts, pharmacy records), the percentage of prescribed doses actually taken, and the number of patients with poor adherence (less than 80%). Moderating effects should be analyzed by testing whether dose level or adherence moderates the FC-response relationship—interaction terms should be added to regression models if there is sufficient variability.

If adherence data are not available, this should be acknowledged as a limitation. The Methods section should add details on dose titration protocol and adherence monitoring. A new Results subsection titled "Treatment Exposure and Adherence" should be added, along with a new supplementary table called "Final Sertraline Doses and Adherence Rates."

This is important because inadequate medication exposure is a common cause of non-response. If dose or adherence varies substantially and moderates prediction, the FC measure may actually be a proxy for tolerability rather than a pure neurobiological predictor.

The authors should report dosing and adherence data if available in their response, or acknowledge this as a limitation if the data are not available.

---

### B5. Comorbidity as Moderator Should Be Analyzed

Comorbid anxiety and depression are common in OCD and may moderate both treatment response and neural signatures.

The authors should report comorbidity rates—the percentage with comorbid GAD, social anxiety, panic disorder, the range of BDI scores (the mean is reported, but the distribution is important), and other relevant comorbidities that fell below exclusion thresholds. The manuscript should test whether comorbidity moderates the FC-response relationship by dichotomizing (any comorbid anxiety versus pure OCD) or using continuous measures (BDI score as a moderator). Interaction terms such as FC × comorbidity predicting response should be added. If significant moderation is found, the Discussion should address implications: Does FC predict response only in pure OCD or also with comorbidity? Should future biomarker studies stratify by comorbidity status?

The Methods section should specify how comorbidity was assessed. A new Results subsection titled "Comorbidity as Moderator" should be added. The Discussion should interpret heterogeneity in prediction across comorbidity status. OCD is heterogeneous, and comorbidity-specific biomarkers may have greater clinical utility than one-size-fits-all approaches.

The authors should report comorbidity analyses in their response or explain if the data are unavailable.

---

### B6. Univariate Statistical Approach Should Be Justified

Readers may question why contemporary machine learning methods were not employed, given their prevalence in recent neuroimaging prediction studies.

A new paragraph should be added to the Methods, Statistical Analysis section:

> "Univariate logistic regression was employed rather than multivariate machine learning approaches for three reasons. First, with N=54 participants and relatively few predictors (specific FC connections), complex machine learning models (support vector machines, random forests, deep learning) would face severe overfitting risk despite regularization. The curse of dimensionality dictates that high-dimensional models require substantially larger samples to achieve stable performance. Second, univariate models offer greater interpretability, providing clear effect sizes (odds ratios) and clinical interpretability of FC thresholds. Third, the goal was hypothesis-testing (does sensorimotor FC predict response?) rather than optimal prediction (maximizing accuracy at any interpretability cost). While multivariate approaches may achieve marginally higher accuracy in large samples, the simpler univariate approach is more appropriate for this exploratory sample size and scientific aims. Future studies with larger samples may benefit from comparing univariate and multivariate approaches."

Proactive justification prevents reviewer concerns and demonstrates thoughtful methodological choices. The authors should confirm in their response that this paragraph has been added and consider whether additional justification is needed.

## PART C: OPTIONAL ENHANCEMENTS (Would Strengthen Paper)

### C1. Code and Data Sharing

The authors may wish to consider depositing preprocessing scripts, analysis code, and processed FC matrices (anonymized) in a public repository such as OSF, GitHub, or the journal's data sharing platform. This would enhance reproducibility, allow independent verification, and align with open science principles increasingly valued in neuroimaging research. The authors should indicate their willingness to share materials and the timeline in their response.

---

### C2. Symptom Dimension Analysis

If the data are available, the authors may wish to consider using the Y-BOCS symptom checklist to categorize patients by dominant symptom dimension (contamination, symmetry, hoarding, etc.) and test whether predictive patterns differ across dimensions. OCD heterogeneity is increasingly recognized, and dimension-specific biomarkers may have greater clinical utility than general approaches. The authors should indicate in their response if this is feasible with their data.

---

### C3. Cerebellar Parcellation Analysis

Given the unexpected cerebellar findings, the authors may wish to consider conducting more detailed parcellation that distinguishes sensorimotor cerebellar regions (lobules I-VI) from cognitive regions (Crus I/II, VIIb). This would strengthen the cerebellar findings and provide more specific mechanistic hypotheses about which cerebellar functions relate to treatment response. The authors should indicate their interest and feasibility in their response.

---

### C4. Comparison to Published Prediction Models

The authors may wish to consider creating a systematic comparison table showing study, sample size, method, predictors, and AUC or accuracy for all prior OCD treatment prediction studies. This would contextualize the contribution within the broader literature and help readers assess relative performance. The authors should indicate in their response if they will add this to the Discussion.

---

### C5. Multi-Site Validation Protocol

In the Discussion, the authors may wish to consider outlining a specific plan for external validation—target sample size, recruitment sites, inclusion criteria, and a pre-registered analysis plan. This would demonstrate commitment to validation and provide a clear roadmap for future work. The authors should indicate in their response whether they have plans or collaborations for validation studies.

## EVALUATION SUMMARY

This study addresses an important clinical problem with rigorous methodology. The drug-naïve sample is particularly valuable, and the preprocessing approach is exemplary. The convergence with Wang et al. (2025, *J Affect Disord*) using a different method (zfALFF versus FC) substantially strengthens the biological plausibility of the sensorimotor circuit hypothesis.

The required revisions are substantial but achievable. All essential requirements can be addressed with the existing data. Upon satisfactory revision, this work will make a valuable contribution to the OCD treatment prediction literature and should stimulate important validation efforts.
