### 1. Multi-modal Data Integration Framework: The "Holistic Connectome" Approach

The fundamental challenge in developmental neuroscience lies in the "explanatory gap" between genomic susceptibility and clinical phenotype. To bridge this, our methodology employs a robust **다중 모달 (Multi-modal)** integration framework designed to synthesize heterogeneous data streams into a unified biological manifold.

#### 1.1. Data Acquisition and High-Dimensional Preprocessing (데이터 전처리)
The integrity of AI-driven inference is predicated on the rigor of **데이터 전처리 (Data preprocessing)**. Our pipeline targets four primary streams:

*   **Structural and Functional Neuroimaging (MRI/fMRI):** We will utilize high-resolution T1-weighted, Diffusion Tensor Imaging (DTI), and resting-state fMRI (rs-fMRI) from the ABCD (Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development) Study and the UK Biobank. Preprocessing will involve standardized BIDS-compliant pipelines, including cortical surface reconstruction (FreeSurfer), motion artifact correction using independent component analysis (ICA-AROMA), and geodesic distance mapping to preserve the topological integrity of the cortical mantle [Source 1, Source 3].
*   **Genomic and Epigenomic Profiling:** Beyond simple GWAS-derived Polygenic Risk Scores (PRS), we will integrate Whole Exome Sequencing (WES) and DNA methylation patterns. This allows for the identification of rare variants and epigenetic modifications that modulate synaptic pruning pathways [Source 5].
*   **Clinical and Behavioral Phenomics:** Deep phenotyping will be performed using standardized assessments (e.g., ADOS-2, Vineland-3) integrated with Electronic Health Record (EHR) data. Natural Language Processing (NLP) will be utilized to extract sub-clinical behavioral markers from clinical notes [Source 7].

#### 1.2. Latent Space Alignment and Fusion
Traditional concatenation methods fail to capture the non-linear interactions between genes and circuits. We propose a **Canonical Correlation Analysis (CCA)-enhanced Variational Autoencoder (VAE)**. This architecture projects genomic and imaging data into a shared latent space, where the "biological distance" between a specific genetic mutation (e.g., *SHANK3* deletion) and its corresponding neuro-circuitry disruption (e.g., hypoconnectivity in the prefrontal cortex) can be quantified and modeled as a trajectory of developmental decay [Source 2, Source 4].

### 2. Advanced AI Architecture: Spatio-Temporal Graph Transformers (AI 모델 개발)

The crux of this proposal is the **AI 모델 개발 (AI model development)** of a novel **Spatio-Temporal Graph Transformer (STGT)**. This architecture is uniquely suited to the brain’s non-Euclidean geometry and its dynamic temporal fluctuations.

#### 2.1. Graph Neural Network (GNN) for Structural Topology
The brain is modeled as a graph $G = (V, E)$, where $V$ represents parcellated brain regions (nodes) and $E$ represents white matter tracts (edges). We will implement a **Graph Attention Network (GAT)** to weigh the importance of specific structural hubs (e.g., the Default Mode Network) in predicting developmental outcomes. Unlike standard CNNs, GATs allow for the identification of "vulnerable nodes" within the connectome that are disproportionately affected by genetic insults [Source 6, Source 8].

#### 2.2. Transformer-based Temporal Dynamics
To capture the "chronnectome"—the time-varying nature of brain states—we utilize a Transformer architecture with multi-head self-attention. This allows the model to attend to long-range temporal dependencies in fMRI BOLD signals.
*   **Mechanistic Innovation:** We will introduce a "Biological Inductive Bias" into the Transformer layers, where the attention weights are constrained by the underlying structural graph. This ensures that the AI does not merely find statistical correlations but adheres to the physical constraints of neural signaling.

#### 2.3. Hyper-parameter Optimization and Interpretability
Using SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) and Integrated Gradients, we will "open the black box." This ensures that when the **AI 모델 개발** predicts a high risk for a developmental disorder, it provides a "neurobiological receipt"—pointing specifically to, for example, a failure in the thalamocortical loop or an aberrant pruning process in the temporal lobe [Source 1, Source 2].

### 3. Cross-Species Validation: Zebrafish Models and Clinical Cohorts

A Nobel-tier methodology must move beyond correlation. We propose a "Reverse-Translational" loop that validates AI-generated hypotheses using the *Danio rerio* (zebrafish) model.

#### 3.1. Zebrafish (Danio rerio) Mechanistic Probing
Zebrafish offer 70% genetic homology with humans and a transparent larvae stage, allowing for whole-brain imaging at cellular resolution.
*   **CRISPR/Cas9 Mutagenesis:** Based on the high-impact genetic variants identified by our **다중 모달** AI framework, we will generate corresponding zebrafish mutants (e.g., *cntnap2*, *syngap1*).
*   **Light-Sheet Microscopy:** We will employ *in vivo* calcium imaging (GCaMP6s) to observe neural ensemble dynamics in the "Z-brain." This allows us to test if the AI-predicted "circuit-level resonance" failure is present at the single-neuron level [Source 3, Source 5].
*   **Pharmacological Rescuing:** We will use the zebrafish model for high-throughput screening of small molecules that can normalize the aberrant neural firing patterns identified by the AI, providing a direct path to clinical translation.

#### 3.2. Clinical Cohort Validation and Longitudinal Tracking
The AI model’s predictive power will be prospectively validated in a clinical cohort ($N=500$) of pediatric patients. We will utilize "Digital Phenotyping" (wearable sensors tracking sleep and motor activity) to provide continuous data streams, which will be fed back into the STGT model to refine its longitudinal trajectory predictions [Source 7, Source 8].

### 4. Data Privacy, Ethics, and Federated Learning

Given the sensitivity of pediatric genomic and neuroimaging data, this proposal adheres to the highest ethical standards through a Decentralized AI approach.

#### 4.1. Federated Learning (FL) Protocol
To scale our **AI 모델 개발** without moving sensitive data across borders, we will implement a Federated Learning framework. In this setup, the model "travels" to the data (e.g., at Samsung Medical Center or the Mayo Clinic). Only the encrypted model gradients are shared and aggregated at a central server. This ensures HIPAA and GDPR compliance while maximizing the power of "Big Data" [Source 4, Source 6].

#### 4.2. Differential Privacy and Secure Multi-Party Computation (SMPC)
We will integrate Differential Privacy (DP) by adding calibrated noise to the training gradients, ensuring that no individual patient’s data can be reconstructed from the AI model. SMPC will be used for the **다중 모달** integration phase, allowing genomic data and imaging data to be fused without either party seeing the raw underlying information.

## Budget Justification

The proposed budget for this research initiative is strategically calculated at a total of **101.33억원**, designed to facilitate a paradigm shift in AI-driven drug discovery and precision medicine. As a specialist in scientific resource allocation, I have structured this budget to ensure that every KRW invested yields maximum Research ROI by aligning high-cost infrastructure with critical path milestones. This project requires a sophisticated synergy between large-scale computational power, elite interdisciplinary human capital, and rigorous biological validation. The following sections provide a granular justification for these allocations, ensuring total transparency and alignment with the Samsung Future Tech Grant’s standards for innovation and feasibility.

### 1. Computational Infrastructure: High-Performance GPU Clusters

A primary pillar of the **AI 관련 예산** (AI-related budget) is the acquisition and maintenance of a high-performance computing (HPC) environment centered on NVIDIA H100 and A100 Tensor Core GPU clusters. In the current landscape of deep learning, particularly for generative protein modeling and multi-modal clinical data integration, computational throughput is the fundamental rate-limiting step [Source 1].

#### Necessity of H100/A100 Architectures
The transition from traditional GPU architectures to H100 clusters is justified by the Transformer Engine and the significantly increased memory bandwidth required for Large Language Models (LLMs) and Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) used in molecular docking simulations.
*   **Training Efficiency:** The H100 provides up to 9x faster training for large-scale AI models compared to previous generations. By reducing training cycles from months to weeks, we effectively compress the R&D timeline, allowing for more iterative hypothesis testing [Source 7].
*   **Memory Constraints:** Processing high-resolution 3D cryo-EM data and whole-genome sequences requires the 80GB high-bandwidth memory (HBM3) found in these units. Attempting this research on consumer-grade or aging enterprise hardware would lead to "out-of-memory" errors and significant bottlenecks in model convergence [Source 1].
*   **ROI Justification:** The capital expenditure for these clusters is offset by the reduction in cloud computing egress fees and the long-term value of an on-site, secure environment for proprietary data processing. This ensures that the **101.33억원** investment creates a reusable asset for the institution beyond the initial grant period.

### 2. Personnel Costs: Top-Tier Interdisciplinary Team

The success of a "Future Tech" project rests not on the tools alone, but on the specialized talent capable of bridging the gap between computational theory and biological reality. We have allocated a significant portion of the budget to secure a "dream team" of researchers [Source 2].

#### Interdisciplinary Composition
*   **Principal AI Architects:** These individuals are responsible for designing the bespoke neural architectures. Given the global competition for AI talent, competitive salaries are necessary to retain experts who can implement complex RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) strategies and hybrid modeling [Source 8].
*   **Computational Biologists & Chemists:** These researchers translate biological problems into mathematical frameworks. Their role is to ensure that the AI outputs are chemically feasible and biologically relevant, preventing "hallucinations" in the drug discovery process.
*   **Clinical Liaison Officers:** To ensure the project remains grounded in patient outcomes, we have included senior clinicians. They provide the domain expertise required to label clinical datasets and interpret the clinical significance of AI-generated biomarkers [Source 2].
*   **Ethics and Regulatory Specialists:** Given the sensitivity of clinical data, a dedicated portion of the personnel budget is allocated to compliance officers who ensure that all data handling meets international HIPAA and GDPR-equivalent standards in Korea.

The personnel budget is justified by the "Multiplier Effect": high-caliber experts reduce the likelihood of project failure and significantly increase the impact factor of resulting publications and patents, thereby maximizing the grant's ROI [Source 8].

### 3. Clinical Data Acquisition and Processing

High-quality data is the "fuel" for our AI models. A significant portion of the **AI 관련 예산** is dedicated to the acquisition, cleaning, and longitudinal tracking of clinical data [Source 3].

#### Data Access and Curation
*   **Biobank Access Fees:** We require access to high-fidelity, multi-omic datasets (genomic, proteomic, and transcriptomic) from leading medical centers. These institutions charge substantial fees for the ethical preparation and de-identification of patient data [Source 5].
*   **Data Harmonization:** Clinical data often arrives in unstructured formats (handwritten notes, disparate imaging standards). We have budgeted for advanced OCR (Optical Character Recognition) and NLP (Natural Language Processing) tools to convert this raw information into a machine-readable format.
*   **Longitudinal Tracking:** Unlike static datasets, our methodology requires tracking patient responses over time to validate the AI’s predictive accuracy. This necessitates ongoing partnership fees with clinical trial sites [Source 3].

By investing in high-quality, curated data at the outset, we minimize the "garbage in, garbage out" risk, ensuring that the AI models are trained on ground-truth clinical reality rather than noisy, unverified datasets [Source 5].

### 4. In Vivo Validation: Zebrafish (Danio rerio) Experiments

While AI can predict potential drug candidates, biological validation remains the gold standard for safety and efficacy. We have selected the zebrafish model as our primary *in vivo* platform due to its high throughput and genetic similarity to humans [Source 4].

#### Cost Breakdown for Zebrafish Research
*   **Automated Phenotyping Systems:** To match the speed of AI-generated leads, we require automated imaging platforms that can screen hundreds of zebrafish embryos simultaneously. This equipment is essential for high-content screening (HCS) [Source 6].
*   **CRISPR/Cas9 Reagents:** To validate specific genetic targets identified by our AI, we will generate "knock-out" or "knock-in" zebrafish lines. The cost of high-fidelity guide RNAs and specialized microinjection equipment is included here.
*   **Husbandry and Maintenance:** Maintaining a sterile, temperature-controlled facility for thousands of fish requires specialized staff and continuous monitoring systems.
*   **ROI of Zebrafish vs. Murine Models:** Utilizing zebrafish instead of mice for initial validation significantly reduces costs and ethical hurdles while maintaining a high degree of physiological relevance. This allows us to test a wider array of AI-predicted compounds within the **101.33억원** budget, effectively increasing the "shots on goal" [Source 4, Source 6].

The budget is phased to follow the project's three-year timeline:

*   **Year 3 (Validation & Translation):** The budget shifts toward the zebrafish facility and clinical validation studies to confirm the AI’s findings.
