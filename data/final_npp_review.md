Confidential remarks for the Editor/Publisher:
The authors have made substantial efforts to address the previous reviews, including performing LOOCV, comparing to clinical predictors, and reframing their claims as exploratory. They've also expanded the limitations section. However, the core issue remains: the small sample size (N=54) and single-site nature of the study severely limit the generalizability and impact of the findings. Despite the authors' efforts, the study remains underpowered for robust biomarker discovery. The observed predictive value, even with LOOCV, may still be inflated, and the lack of an independent validation cohort is a critical weakness. While the work is now methodologically sound, it's unlikely to have a significant impact on the field due to these inherent limitations. I recommend rejection, as further revisions would not overcome these fundamental constraints.

Remarks to be sent to the author:
Thank you for your extensive revisions to the manuscript, addressing the concerns raised in the first round of reviews. We appreciate the addition of leave-one-out cross-validation, the comparison to clinical predictors, the reframing of claims as exploratory, and the expansion of the limitations section.

Specifically, we acknowledge the inclusion of:
*   LOOCV to provide more realistic estimates of predictive performance.
*   Clinical-only, FC-only, and combined models to assess the incremental value of neuroimaging.
*   A shift in language to emphasize the exploratory nature of the findings.
*   Expanded discussion of limitations, including generalizability and sample size concerns.

However, despite these improvements, the fundamental limitation of the small sample size (N=54) and single-site design persists. While the cross-validation mitigates some overfitting concerns, the study remains underpowered to identify robust and generalizable biomarkers. The lack of an independent validation cohort further limits the clinical translatability of the findings.

Therefore, we regret to inform you that we are unable to accept the manuscript for publication in Neuropsychopharmacology. While the study is now methodologically sound and addresses many of the previous concerns, the inherent limitations related to sample size and generalizability preclude the level of impact and robustness required for publication in our journal. We wish you success in publishing this work elsewhere.
