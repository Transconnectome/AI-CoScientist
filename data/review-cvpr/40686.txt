Image Classification from Randomized and Deterministic EEG Trials
Anonymous CVPR submission
Abstract
In this paper, we propose a new deep model, TS- 001
SpectrumNet, to achieve improved image classification from 002
both randomized and deterministic EEG trials, which is ex- 003
tremely challenging and the best classification rate reported 004
so far is only 7.0% for randomized trials and 48.1% for de- 005
terministic trials. To confront the challenge brought in by 006
randomized EEG trials and semantics ambiguity inside nat- 007
ural stimuli images, we propose two new modules: TSDL 008
(Temporal-Spatial Dynamics Learning) and SpecRL (Spec- 009
trum Representational Learning) to achieve the best pos- 010
sible exploitation of the classifiable information embedded 011
inside EEG trials. While the first module is introduced to 012
simultaneously learn temporal-spatial dynamics with multi- 013
ple convolutional kernels to accommodate a broader range 014
of varying characteristics of both the randomized and de- 015
terministic EEG trials, the second module is introduced to 016
achieve representational learning in spectrum domain to 017
characterize those hierarchical global correlations beyond 018
the constraint of localities. In this way, our proposed TS- 019
SpectrumNet is able to achieve not only the comprehen- 020
sive capture of complex dynamics inside EEG sequences 021
but also the full exploitation of spectrum features across all 022
temporal-spatial EEG channels. To minimize the impact of 023
noises, we further introduce a channel-wise normalization 024
scheme to optimize the quality of EEG input for our pro- 025
posed TS-SpectrumNet. Extensive experiments are carried 026
out and the results validate that: (i) over randomized EEG 027
trials, our proposed delivers a classification rate of 15.1%, 028
more than doubling the best rate reported so far; and (ii) 029
over deterministic EEG trials, our proposed delivers the 030
best classification rate of 50.3% at the cost of 0.1 million 031
parameters, whist the existing best performing EEGChan- 032
nelNet achieves the classification rate at 48.1% at the cost 033
of 5 million parameters.034

3.2. TSDL: Temporal-Spatial Dynamics Learning 270
The temporal-spatial dynamics learning (TSDL) module 271
is the first core processing stage of our network, applied 272
directly after the channel-wise normalization of the raw 273
96×512EEG input for randomized EEG-ImageNet and 274
the raw128×512input for deterministic EEG-ImageNet. 275
The primary objective of this module is to capture the rich 276spatio-temporal dynamics inherent in EEG signals across277
multiple time scales simultaneously. As illustrated in Fig-278
ure 2, the TSDL module employs a parallel, multi-branch279
architecture inspired by the ”Inception” concept, allowing280
the network to learn from different temporal receptive fields281
concurrently [16].282
The normalized input,X norm∈RC×T, is fed into three283
independent 1D convolutional branches, each with a differ-284
ent kernel size to sustain effective learning across the vary-285
ing aspects of the distinct spatio-temporal dynamics, which286
can be highlighted as follows.287
•Small Kernel (S) Branch:This branch utilizes 1D con-288
volutions with a small kernel size (k S= 63), in order289
to enable the learning module to capture high-frequency,290
local, and fine-grained spatio-temporal dynamics.291
•Medium Kernel (M) Branch:This branch uses 1D con-292
volutions with a medium kernel size (k M= 127). This293
is aimed at enabling the dynamics learning to focus on294
those temporal dependencies and spatial correlations over295
medium sized time windows.296
•Large Kernel (L) Branch:This branch is constructed297
by a number of 1D convolution units with a large kernel298
size (k L= 255) to empower the dynamics learning with299
wider receptive fields, which is crucial for capturing low-300
frequency, long-range temporal dependencies and global301
spatio-temporal relationships across the EEG sequences.302
As shown in Figure 2 (e.g., M1, M2, ..., M8), each303
branch consists of multiple parallel convolutional filters.304
The 1D convolution operation in each filter is ”spatio-305
temporal” as it spans allCinput channels while convolv-306
ing along the temporal dimensionT, thus learning patterns307
that involve correlationsbetweenchannelsovera specific308
duration. The outputs of the parallel filters within a sin-309
gle branch are then aggregated via element-wise summation310
(indicated by the symbolL) to produce a single but robust311
feature map for that specific scale.312
