Abstract
Capturing dynamic spatiotemporal neural activity is essen- 001
tial for understanding large-scale brain mechanisms. Func- 002
tional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) provides high- 003
resolution cortical representations that form a strong ba- 004
sis for characterizing fine-grained brain activity patterns. 005
The high acquisition cost of fMRI limits large-scale ap- 006
plications, therefore making high-quality fMRI reconstruc- 007
tion a crucial task. Electroencephalography (EEG) of- 008
fers millisecond-level temporal cues that complement fMRI. 009
Leveraging this complementarity, we present an EEG- 010
conditioned framework for reconstructing dynamic fMRI as 011
continuous neural sequences with high spatial fidelity and 012
strong temporal coherence at the cortical-vertex level. To 013
address sampling irregularities common in real fMRI acqui- 014
sitions, we incorporate a null-space intermediate-frame re- 015
construction, enabling measurement-consistent completion 016
of arbitrary intermediate frames and improving sequence 017
continuity and practical applicability. Experiments on the 018
CineBrain dataset demonstrate superior voxel-wise recon- 019
struction quality and robust temporal consistency across 020
whole-brain and functionally specific regions. The recon- 021
structed fMRI also preserves essential functional informa- 022
tion, supporting downstream visual decoding tasks. This 023
work provides a new pathway for estimating high-resolution 024
fMRI dynamics from EEG and advances multimodal neu- 025
roimaging toward more dynamic brain activity modeling. 0261. Introduction027
Understanding brain dynamics requires capturing both high028
spatial detail and temporal continuity [1]. Functional mag-029
netic resonance imaging (fMRI) measures neural activ-030
ity through blood-oxygen-level–dependent (BOLD) signals031
and provides fine-grained spatial resolution for studying032
cognition and brain function [2, 3]. Each fMRI frame re-033
flects an ongoing state of brain activity, and together these034
frames form a continuous temporal sequence. The high ac-035
quisition cost of fMRI limits large-scale applications, there-036
fore making high-quality fMRI reconstruction a crucial037
task. However, conventional analyses and many reconstruc-038
tion methods treat frames independently [4–6], overlook-039
ing the rich temporal dependencies that govern how neural040
patterns emerge, evolve, and interact. Modeling these dy-041
namics is inherently challenging, as it requires reconstruct-042
ing high-dimensional spatiotemporal sequences that remain043
both temporally coherent and spatially detailed.044
This raises a central question:how can we reconstruct045
fMRI frames that are both temporally coherent and spa-046
tially detailed, capturing the brain’s continuous dynamics047
rather than isolated snapshots?To address this challenge,048
we turn to electroencephalography (EEG), which captures049
neural activity with millisecond temporal resolution [7],050
complementing the spatial precision of fMRI. By leverag-051
ing EEG as a conditioning signal, we can guide the recon-052
struction of dynamic fMRI frames that preserve temporal053
continuity across frames. Beyond reconstruction fidelity,054

2. Related Work116
2.1. EEG-to-fMRI Translation117
EEG-to-fMRI translation methods can be divided into ROI-118
level sequence modeling and voxel or surface-level recon-119
struction. ROI-based methods such as NeuroBOLT [8] re-120
construct fMRI sequences at the parcel level. These meth-121
ods naturally capture temporal continuity across frames but122
lose spatial precision because parcellation removes fine cor-123
tical detail. V oxel-level or cortical-level approaches, such124
as CNN TC [9], CNN TAG [5], adversarial models includ-125
ing E2fGAN and E2fNet [10], and CATD [6] operate in126
volumetric or cortical space. They achieve higher spatial127
fidelity but typically reconstruct each fMRI frame indepen-128
dently. As a result, these methods lack explicit modeling of129
temporal coherence across the sequence.130
Evaluation protocols follow the same pattern. ROI-level131
methods mainly report correlation-based metrics between132
predicted and ground-truth time series. V oxel-level meth-133
ods commonly rely on image reconstruction metrics such as134
MSE, correlation, SSIM, and PSNR. These metrics reflect135
reconstruction fidelity but do not reveal whether the recon-136
structed fMRI contains functional information that can sup-137
port downstream applications such as intermediate-frame138
completion or neural decoding.139
Our work addresses these gaps. We introduce an EEG-140
conditioned diffusion framework that models spatial detail141
and temporal consistency jointly at vertex-level resolution.142
We further incorporate a null space-based strategy that en-143
ables measurement-consistent reconstruction of intermedi-144
ate frames without retraining. More importantly, we evalu-145
ate whether the reconstructed fMRI preserves task-relevant146
neural representations that support downstream functional147
applications.148
2.2. Diffusion Models149
Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs) [14,150
15] have demonstrated strong capabilities in modeling com-151
plex data distributions, achieving remarkable success in im-152
age generation [16]. Over time, diffusion models have153
evolved into a flexible probabilistic modeling framework154
whose iterative denoising process naturally captures multi-155
scale spatial structure and complex data manifolds while of-156
fering stable training dynamics. Subsequent works, such157

5. Conclusion and Discussion513
Conclusion.In this work, we formulate EEG-to-fMRI 514
reconstruction simultaneously as a spatiotemporal pattern 515
recognition problem, where the model extracts neurally 516
meaningful dynamic features from EEG, and as a gener- 517
ative modeling task, where it reconstructs dynamic fMRI 518
frames in high spatial resolution. Within a subject-specific 519
setting, our diffusion-transformer-based framework recon- 520
structs fMRI trajectories that are spatially detailed, tem- 521porally coherent, and consistent with known physiological522
dynamics. Our model achieves substantially better perfor-523
mance than previous representative baseline. To support524
intermediate frame reconstruction, we incorporate a null-525
space constrained sampling strategy that enforces consis-526
tency with observed frames during the generative process.527
This enables the model to infer missing temporal frames528
without retraining and provides a principled way to assess529
temporal consistency. Functional validation through task-530
driven visual decoding shows that the reconstructed fMRI531
frames preserve discriminative neural representations rather532
than only matching low-level similarity metrics. Overall,533
this study demonstrates a feasible path to unify the temporal534
precision of EEG with the high spatial resolution of fMRI535
for dynamic brain activity modeling, cross-modal neural re-536
construction, and future applications in neural decoding.537
Discussion.While this framework achieves strong perfor-538
mance, two challenges remain. (1) fMRI responses during539
complex naturalistic tasks exhibit substantial cross-subject540
variability, making cross-subject modeling highly challeng-541
ing. Our model is therefore trained within each subject,542
and extending it to subject-independent settings will require543
stronger anatomical or functional alignment. (2) We adopt a544
fixed EEG–fMRI delay following standard practice, yet the545
true hemodynamic latency is region-dependent and time-546
varying. Although our dynamic sequence modeling par-547
tially alleviates this mismatch, future work is needed to in-548
tegrate adaptive or learnable alignment.549
